This is the prompt I would give Antigravity. It is **very focused**—it doesn't ask it to redesign your system, only to improve the **quality and credibility** of the recommendations.

---

## Prompt

My Recommendation Engine is working correctly from an architectural perspective. **Do not modify the backend architecture, deterministic scoring pipeline, APIs, database schema, candidate generation flow, or checkout integration.**

I only want to improve the **quality, relevance, and credibility** of the recommendations and their explanations.

### 1. Improve Recommendation Relevance

The recommended product should feel like something the user would genuinely consider purchasing.

When selecting the highest-scoring candidate, prioritize products that:

* naturally complement the current cart,
* align with the user's purchase history,
* match the user's persona,
* belong to an unexplored category,
* are realistic cross-sell opportunities.

Avoid recommendations that technically satisfy the scoring rules but feel random or weak.

---

### 2. Improve Explanation Quality

The explanation should never sound generic.

Avoid sentences such as:

* "Helps you discover a new category."
* "Fits your preferred categories."
* "Naturally complements your routine."

Instead, generate explanations that explicitly reference the deterministic scoring inputs.

For example:

* "You frequently purchase Ready-to-Eat meals and breakfast products."
* "Your current cart already contains everyday grocery essentials."
* "Snacks & Munchies is one of the few categories you haven't explored yet."
* "This is an affordable first purchase that expands your shopping basket with minimal risk."

The explanation must be grounded in the actual persona, cart, purchase history, and matched reasons passed from Python.

Do not invent user behaviour.

---

### 3. Improve "Matched Because"

Instead of generic reasons, display specific deterministic reasons such as:

✓ Complements products already in your cart

✓ Matches your Quality Seeker shopping behaviour

✓ Expands into an unexplored category

✓ Frequently purchased alongside similar products

Only display reasons that are actually true for the recommendation.

---

### 4. Improve Match Percentage

The current match percentages (95–99%) feel unrealistic.

Instead:

* derive the percentage from the normalized deterministic score,
* use a wider distribution,
* reserve 95%+ only for exceptionally strong matches.

Typical ranges should look like:

* 65–75% → Moderate match
* 76–85% → Strong match
* 86–94% → Excellent match
* 95%+ → Rare, near-perfect match

Do not generate arbitrary percentages.

---

### 5. Improve Candidate Selection

If multiple products receive similar scores, prefer the product that:

1. Best complements the current cart.
2. Feels like a natural cross-sell.
3. Requires the least behavioural leap for the user.
4. Represents a realistic Blinkit recommendation.

The recommendation should feel intentional rather than random.

---

### 6. Ensure Internal Consistency

The following must always agree:

* Persona
* Purchase History
* Current Cart
* Recommendation
* Matched Reasons
* LLM Explanation
* Match Percentage

There should never be contradictions such as recommending a Snacks product while claiming it matches only Breakfast preferences.

---

### 7. Preserve Existing Architecture

Do **not** modify:

* backend APIs,
* deterministic scoring logic,
* RAG,
* Pinecone,
* database,
* scheduler,
* checkout flow,
* modal UI.

Only improve the recommendation quality, explanation quality, and presentation while keeping the existing architecture intact.

---

This prompt focuses on the three areas that will have the biggest impact in a demo:

1. **Better recommendations**
2. **More believable explanations**
3. **More credible match scores**

Those changes will make your recommendation system feel much more like something a production e-commerce platform would use.
